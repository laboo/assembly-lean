# The Strict Core: Rulebook (draft, not locked)

Model v3.0.0. Drafted Oct 16, 2026. Nothing here is locked until the author approves it.

## How the core works

The core holds only the rules listed below, and each rule comes from a sentence of the model.

**What you can put in.** A particle, named by a floor and that floor's numbers, and an experiment, named by its looks and turns. Nothing else can be put in: no speeds, no extra circles, no copies.

**What it measures.** The core works out the odds and seals them before anyone opens the observed numbers. Then the two are compared.

**Locks and records.**
- The core is locked by its fingerprint. Any change breaks the fingerprint and needs a new model version with the author's go.
- Every try is written to a log in which each entry carries the fingerprint of the one before it. Editing or deleting a try breaks the chain, and failures are logged like passes.
- Where the model is silent, the core does not fill the gap. It stops and says "the model doesn't say."

## Rules

1. **Floors.** A floor is a count of directions: the point floor has 0, then the line, the plane, ours (3), the fourth, and up. Each floor holds the floors below it as its first directions. (The Building.)
2. **Whole numbers only.** Every number is a whole number, or a whole-number pair for the complex plane, as in Lean pieces 1 to 8. Odds come out as two whole numbers, a part and a whole. No decimals appear anywhere in the core. (The Building; The Machine.)
3. **A particle's numbers.** A particle has one number for each direction of its floor, at every place of our world. A particle on the point floor has just its one number. *Reading; needs the author's ruling.* (Numbers and Particles.)
4. **Turns.** No one sets how anything turns.
   - A turn of our world turns the same directions on every floor that has them, and leaves any extra directions alone.
   - The plane floor sits across the particle's path, and a turn carries it along with the path.

   This replaces v3.0.0's per-floor table of turning speeds. *Needs the author's ruling.* (Turning: "nothing in the building actually spins.")
5. **Odds.** The odds of an answer are the squared length of the particle's shadow on that answer's plane, out of its whole squared length. That the odds add to one is proved once, in Lean. (Landing and Chance.)
6. **Looks.** The core builds a look from a direction in our world, and there are only two kinds.
   - A *split* gives the part along the direction and the part across it, as a polarizer does.
   - A *sort* groups points by how they turn about the direction, as a magnet does.

   The experiment's record says which kind of look was used; the tester never chooses. (Standard physics also lets the device decide; a tie.) *Needs the author's ruling.* (Landing and Chance: "the planes of its possible answers.")
7. **Mirror.** A particle's mirror image flips every number to its mirror, as in Lean piece 5. (Signs and Mirrors.)
8. **Several particles.** Several particles together are one object on a floor whose directions are their arrangements. (Numbers and Particles.)

## Hard stops (the model doesn't say, so the core refuses)

- **How numbers move between neighbors.** There is no step rule in v3.0.0. This blocks mass, the positron check, the double slit and charge.
- **Charge.** Its circle sits on no floor.
- **Which particles flip sign when two of them swap.** The model says only that electrons do.
- **Where the line floor sits inside ours.**
- **"The four-wide numbers."** They aren't a floor, so they can't be put in.
- **Events,** the only place where opposite marks are said to cancel.
- **A plane-floor particle with no path,** that is, one at rest.

## What can run now

The odds of looks, before and after turns of our world, for any particle placed on the point, plane, ours or a higher floor. That covers the Higgs, light, the graviton, the W and Z, and the electron's spin.
